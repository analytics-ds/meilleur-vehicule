> **In brief:**
> 1. Oil changes are needed every 10,000 to 15,000 km depending on engine type.
> 2. The timing belt must be changed between 100,000 and 160,000 km.
> 3. Not following the maintenance logbook can void the manufacturer's warranty.

## Why follow a maintenance schedule

Regular maintenance extends the vehicle's lifespan, limits costly breakdowns, and often conditions the continuation of the manufacturer's warranty.

## Interventions by mileage

### The reference schedule

| Mileage | Intervention | Frequency |
|---|---|---|
| 10,000 - 15,000 km | Oil and oil filter change | Annual |
| 20,000 - 30,000 km | Brake pads | Depending on wear |
| 40,000 - 60,000 km | Fuel filter, spark plugs | Depending on engine |
| 100,000 - 160,000 km | Timing belt | Every 5 to 10 years |

## Signs that require an immediate check

- Engine warning light on continuously
- Metallic noise when braking
- Abnormal vibrations in the steering wheel

## Keeping invoices for resale

An up-to-date maintenance logbook, backed by invoices, adds value to the vehicle when reselling and reassures the buyer. See our [used car buying checklist](/en/blog/buying-used-car-checklist/) for what a buyer checks.

## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>How many kilometers between oil changes?</summary>

An oil change is generally needed every 10,000 to 15,000 km or once a year, depending on the engine type and the manufacturer's recommendation.

</details>

<details>
<summary>When should the timing belt be changed?</summary>

Between 100,000 and 160,000 km, or every 5 to 10 years depending on the model. A belt failure while driving can cause costly engine damage.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Does failing to follow the maintenance schedule void the warranty?</summary>

Yes, most manufacturers make the warranty conditional on strictly following the maintenance schedule, with invoices as proof.

</details>
