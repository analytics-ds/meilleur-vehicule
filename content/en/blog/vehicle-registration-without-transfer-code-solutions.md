> **In brief**
> 1. Missing a transfer code does not block registration: **three channels** exist, **two** of which require no code.
> 2. On the government website, you then need the **document number** and **issue date** of the crossed-out registration certificate. Without a transfer code, supplying documents becomes mandatory there and processing times lengthen considerably.
> 3. A vehicle bought from a professional seller **generally does not** generate a transfer code: that is normal, not an error.
> 4. At an approved professional such as cartegrise.com (approved since **2009**, more than **1 million files** processed), the code is not requested.

A Clio bought from a private seller found online, a meeting in a car park, the crossed-out registration certificate and the transfer certificate handed over in person. The seller was supposed to send the transfer code "that same evening". A week later, he no longer answers the phone, and the online process comes to a halt at the code step.

The transfer code simplifies the process, but it is not essential. Depending on the situation, the official channel remains open using other information, or an approved professional takes over. Here is which solution to choose, case by case, keeping in mind that the one-month deadline after purchase keeps running in the meantime.

## The seller did not declare the sale

This is the most common case. The seller must declare the transfer within 15 days of the sale, under article R322-4 of the French Highway Code (Code de la route). It is this declaration, made online, that generates the transfer code to be passed on to the buyer. As long as it has not been made, there is no code.

Two options are open to the buyer.

- **Chase the seller.** A written message, recalling the obligation and the 15-day deadline, is often enough. The seller has their own interest in declaring the sale: as long as they haven't, any traffic offence notices linked to the vehicle keep being sent to them.
- **Move forward without them.** On the government website, the application remains possible without a code, using the document number and issue date of the crossed-out registration certificate. At an approved professional, the code is not requested.

The choice depends on how much time is available. If the seller replies within the day, the code settles everything. If they stay silent, waiting eats into the one-month deadline. The documents the seller must hand over on the day of the sale are recalled in our article on the [mandatory documents to sell your car](/en/blog/selling-car-private-mandatory-documents/).

## The code has been lost or has expired

The official response from France Titres-ANTS dated 2 October 2023, published on the Services Publics+ platform, sets out three points: the transfer code has 5 digits, it remains valid for 15 days, and it cannot be recovered once lost.

A code jotted on a piece of paper that has since been mislaid, or passed on too late, is therefore no longer usable. The seller cannot make it reappear, and the buyer cannot request it from ANTS.

The same official response points to the fallback route: the buyer can apply without a code on the government website, by entering the document number and issue date of the crossed-out registration certificate handed over by the seller. Both pieces of information appear on the certificate itself. This route costs time: without a transfer code, supplying documents is mandatory on the ANTS website, and processing times lengthen considerably.

At an approved professional, a lost or expired code changes nothing: they submit the file directly into the vehicle registration system, without going through the code. [cartegrise.com](https://www.cartegrise.com) covers this case on its page on [registering without a transfer code](https://www.cartegrise.com/code-de-cession).

## Purchase from a professional: why there is no code

Buying a car from a garage or a dealership sometimes surprises buyers used to sales between private individuals: no transfer code is handed over. This is not an error.

The transfer code is generated by the online sale declaration made by a private individual. A motor trade professional follows their own channel, and therefore generally does not generate a code. Many approved professionals take care of the buyer's registration themselves at the time of delivery, sometimes for a billed service.

Two checks are enough at the time of purchase: finding out whether the garage carries out the process, and, if it does not, obtaining the sale documents needed to do it yourself. ## The crossed-out registration certificate is incomplete or mislaid

The crossed-out registration certificate is the central document in the file. The seller must hand it over in full, crossed out with the words "Vendu le" (Sold on) followed by the date and their signature. On a recent registration certificate, the detachable coupon allows the buyer to drive in France for 1 month, giving them time to obtain the new certificate in their name.

When it is missing or incomplete, the official channel without a code becomes difficult: it is on this document that the document number and issue date can be read. Two reflexes to have.

- **Contact the seller again.** They may have a photo or a copy of the registration certificate, which at least makes it possible to retrieve this information.
- **Hand the file to an approved professional.** They review the available documents and indicate which ones still need to be supplied before registering the application.

This case illustrates the main advantage of a professional: the file is checked before it is submitted, which avoids a rejection after the fact.
