> **In brief:**
> 1. 4 documents should always be requested: registration certificate, roadworthiness test, no-liens certificate, maintenance logbook.
> 2. The roadworthiness test must be less than 6 months old for any vehicle over 4 years.
> 3. A test drive of at least 15 minutes reveals most common mechanical defects.

## Why a checklist is essential

Buying a used car remains the riskiest purchase on the car market for an individual: no manufacturer warranty, a history that is sometimes unclear, and a seller who does not always have an interest in revealing everything. A structured checklist limits the risk of mechanical or administrative surprises.

## Documents to check before anything else

### The registration certificate

It must match the vehicle exactly (serial number, plate) and be crossed out with the mention "sold on" followed by the date and the seller's signature.

- Compliant, crossed-out registration certificate
- Roadworthiness test less than 6 months old
- No-liens certificate less than 15 days old
- Up-to-date maintenance logbook

## Mechanical inspection

### What to test during the test drive

A drive of at least 15 minutes, on the road and at different speeds, reveals suspicious noises, gearbox jolts or brake pulling.

| Checkpoint | What it reveals | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Exhaust smoke | Engine condition | Thick blue or white smoke |
| Steering play | Steering wear | More than 2 cm of play on the wheel |
| Marks under the vehicle | Leaks | Oil or coolant stains |

## After the purchase: registration

Once the purchase is completed, the new owner has a legal deadline of one month to register the vehicle in their name. See our guide on [ownership transfer procedures](/en/categories/administrative-procedures/) for the full process.

<summary>What documents should you ask for before buying a used car?</summary>
