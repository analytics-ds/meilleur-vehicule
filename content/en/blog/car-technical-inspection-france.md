> **In brief:**
> 1. The technical inspection covers 133 checkpoints grouped into 9 functions, with a first visit due within 6 months before the vehicle's 4th anniversary, then renewal every 2 years.
> 2. The average price observed in 2026 is around 78 euros, with unregulated rates ranging from 45 to 130 euros depending on the region and the chosen center.
> 3. A major defect allows 2 months for a re-inspection, while a critical defect immobilizes the vehicle until it is repaired.
> 4. Since January 1, 2026, serious manufacturer safety recalls, such as Takata airbags, are checked and can trigger an automatic re-inspection for a critical defect.

The technical inspection is a mandatory step for any owner of a passenger car or camper van under 3.5 tonnes, regardless of the engine type (petrol, diesel, hybrid or electric). It determines whether the vehicle can keep being driven legally, whether it can be resold without unpleasant surprises, and, since 2026, it includes a stricter check of safety recalls declared by manufacturers. Here is what matters before booking an appointment: frequency, checkpoints, average price and the consequences of a failed item.

## Who Needs a Technical Inspection, and How Often

The periodic technical inspection applies to all category M1 vehicles, meaning passenger cars and camper vans with a gross vehicle weight not exceeding 3.5 tonnes. The requirement applies regardless of the energy source: a fully electric or hybrid model must pass the technical inspection just like a combustion-engine vehicle, even though some emissions-related checkpoints do not apply to it.

### The schedule to follow

The first inspection must take place within the 6 months preceding the vehicle's 4th anniversary of first registration. Once that first deadline has passed, renewal is required every 2 years, with no exception based on mileage. Collector vehicles, registered for more than 30 years, benefit from a special regime: the technical inspection is only required every 5 years.

Before selling a vehicle, it is worth checking that the current technical inspection report is still valid, since a report less than 6 months old is one of the [mandatory documents for selling a car privately](/en/blog/selling-car-private-mandatory-documents/) between individuals.

## The 133 Checkpoints and the 9 Inspected Functions

The approved inspector examines the vehicle across 133 checkpoints, grouped into 9 main functions:

- **Vehicle identification**: plate, serial number, consistency with the registration certificate
- **Braking**: condition of discs, pads, system efficiency
- **Steering**: play, mountings, power assistance
- **Visibility**: windshield, wipers, mirrors
- **Lighting and signalling**: headlights, taillights, indicators
- **Road-holding**: tires, shock absorbers, suspension
- **Structure and bodywork**: corrosion, anchor points, doors
- **Equipment**: seatbelts, horn, airbags
- **Pollution and noise level**: emissions depending on the engine, engine noise

Each checkpoint can lead to one of three outcomes: compliant, minor defect, or a more serious defect. A well-maintained vehicle, whose wear has been anticipated through the [car maintenance service schedule](/en/blog/car-maintenance-service-schedule/), significantly reduces the risk of a defect on the most common mechanical checkpoints, such as braking or road-holding.

## Minor, Major and Critical Defects: What Are the Consequences

Not every anomaly found results in a failed inspection. The technical inspection classifies each defect into three severity levels, each with very different consequences.

### Minor defect

A minor defect is a flaw with no significant impact on safety or the environment. It requires neither mandatory repair nor a re-inspection: the technical inspection is passed, but the flaw is simply noted on the report for information.

### Major defect

A major defect compromises the vehicle's proper functioning or represents a potential danger, without requiring immediate immobilization. The owner then has 2 months to repair the flagged item and present the vehicle for a re-inspection. If that deadline is missed, a full technical inspection must be redone, at the owner's expense.

### Critical defect

A critical defect represents an immediate danger to road safety or a serious consequence for the environment. In that case, the validity of the technical inspection is limited to the day of the visit itself: the vehicle can legally be driven again only once the repair has been made and checked by the inspector, with a re-inspection required within 2 months.

A dashboard warning light appearing before the inspection is often a good early indicator of an issue worth addressing beforehand. The guide to [dashboard warning lights](/en/blog/dashboard-warning-lights-meaning/) helps distinguish alerts that need urgent attention from those that can wait until the next service appointment.

## Technical Inspection Price in 2026: Averages, Gaps and the Official Comparison Tool

The price of the technical inspection is entirely unregulated in France: there is no fixed national rate, and each approved center sets its own prices. In 2026, the average price observed for a petrol or diesel passenger car is around 78 euros, but gaps remain significant, from 45 to 130 euros depending on the region, the engine type and the commercial policy of the chosen center.

### The official prix.conso.gouv.fr comparison tool

The French Ministry of the Economy, through the Directorate General for Competition, Consumer Affairs and Fraud Control (DGCCRF), has launched a free comparison tool at prix.conso.gouv.fr. This platform lists the rates charged by more than 5,800 approved centers across the country, with an interactive map to compare prices by department and engine type.

Some departments post particularly competitive rates, such as Loiret, Moselle or Loire, where the average price observed drops below 70 euros. Conversely, some urban or touristic areas charge noticeably higher rates.

### How to lower the bill

A few simple habits help reduce the cost: checking the official comparison tool before choosing a center, booking online rather than walking in (a discount of 5 to 15 euros is common), and watching for the promotional offers regularly run by networks of approved centers.

## What Is Changing With the Technical Inspection From 2026 Onward

The main change in 2026 concerns the check of safety recalls declared by manufacturers.

> "Since January 1, 2026, the inspector checks whether the vehicle is affected by a serious safety recall declared by the manufacturer."
> Source: Service-Public.fr, 2026

In practice, only recalls classified as stop drive trigger an automatic failure. The most well-known case concerns Takata airbags, responsible for several serious accidents worldwide due to a risk of the gas generator exploding. If a vehicle is still fitted with a Takata airbag subject to a stop drive recall, the technical inspection automatically results in a critical defect, with immediate immobilization until the part is replaced by the manufacturer's network.

Beyond safety recalls, the 2026 technical inspection also tightens thresholds on three existing checkpoints: pollutant emissions, with lower particulate and nitrogen oxide thresholds for direct-injection diesel and petrol engines, noise level, and tire condition, particularly tread wear. Clogged particulate filters are also penalized more heavily than before.

Before booking an appointment, it is advisable to check whether the vehicle is affected by an ongoing recall, by entering its vehicle identification number (VIN) on the manufacturer's website or on the rappel.conso.gouv.fr platform.

## What to Do After a Failed Item: The 3-Step Method

Receiving a report with one or more defects is nothing exceptional. Here is how to resolve the situation within the deadline.

1. **Identify the flagged items precisely** on the report handed over by the inspector, distinguishing minor defects (no action required) from major and critical ones (repair and re-inspection mandatory).
2. **Have the vehicle repaired** at a garage, or by yourself if skills and tools allow it, keeping invoices or proof of the parts replaced.
3. **Return to the same center**, or to another approved center, before the 2-month deadline expires. The re-inspection only covers the items that failed, which generally makes it faster and cheaper than a full inspection.

Anticipating these repairs early, particularly when [buying a used car](/en/blog/buying-used-car-checklist/), helps avoid unpleasant surprises and unexpected costs right after a transaction.
