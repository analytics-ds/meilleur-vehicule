> **In brief**
> 1. A vehicle bought without a registration certificate in the buyer's name cannot be driven: the **provisional certificate**, valid for **1 month**, unblocks the situation while waiting for the definitive document.
> 2. An authorized professional accesses the registration system directly and is **not blocked** by an outage of the government website.
> 3. The **one-month** deadline after the purchase and the **15 days** to declare the transfer keep running during the outage: they are not suspended.
> 4. cartegrise.com announces application processing and the provisional certificate **within 12 to 24 hours** after receiving the complete application, with over **1 million applications** processed since 2009.

A used car has just been bought, the transfer certificate is signed, the crossed-out registration certificate is in the glove box. All that is left is the online application, and the ANTS website is not responding. The vehicle stays in the garage, while the legal deadline keeps running.

The real problem is therefore not the outage itself: it is an immobilized vehicle, insurance to arrange and a deadline getting closer. This article starts from these concrete situations, explains what the provisional certificate allows, what happens with insurance, vehicle inspection and the transfer, and how to prepare the application so as not to start from scratch.

## Driving during the outage: what the provisional certificate allows

The provisional certificate of registration is handed over at the end of the application, along with the application number and the registration acknowledgement. According to the service-public.gouv.fr page on change of owner, it allows driving for 1 month, in France only, while waiting for the definitive registration certificate sent by mail.

This document must be printed and kept in the vehicle. It replaces the registration certificate for its period of validity, no more and no less: it does not allow leaving the country, and it cannot be extended.

The question during an outage is therefore simple: who can issue this certificate when the government website is unavailable? A professional authorized by the Ministry of the Interior enters the application directly into the vehicle registration system, without going through the public online service. The interface being down therefore does not prevent them from issuing the provisional certificate.

At [cartegrise.com](https://www.cartegrise.com), application processing and the provisional certificate are announced within 12 to 24 hours after receiving the complete application, and the definitive registration certificate then arrives about 2 to 3 days later. Some applications, other than a change of owner, may take up to 8 business days. Service fees for a change of owner range from €44.90 to €99.90 depending on the regional tax, on top of the taxes, which are the same as through the official channel.

## Insurance, vehicle inspection and transfer during an outage

Three deadlines keep running while the website is unavailable. None of them is suspended.

### Insurance

The application requires certifying on your honor that the owner holds proof of insurance for the vehicle. No copy needs to be attached, but the statement must be accurate: the policy must be taken out before the application, not after. A website outage does not prevent insuring the vehicle, and it is actually a good time to settle this point.

### Vehicle inspection

For a vehicle over 4 years old that is not exempt, the service-public.gouv.fr page requires proof of a vehicle inspection less than 6 months old, or less than 2 months old if a re-inspection was ordered. This is the trickiest deadline during an outage: an inspection report carried out five months before the sale can become too old if the application waits several weeks. It is better to check its date before deciding to wait for the website to come back.

### The transfer declaration

On the seller's side, article R322-4 of the Highway Code requires declaring the transfer within 15 days of the sale. The deadline does not depend on the website's status. For the buyer, the stakes are concrete: the seller's declaration produces the transfer code, which simplifies the application. Without it, the official channel requires additional supporting documents, as detailed in our article on [registration without a transfer code](/en/blog/vehicle-registration-without-transfer-code-solutions/).

On the buyer's side, the one-month deadline to put the registration certificate in your own name also runs from the sale, as service-public.gouv.fr reminds. An outage of a few days is not a problem; an outage that falls at the end of this month is.

## Prepare your application now to avoid starting from scratch

An outage leaves time to gather a complete application, which avoids a back-and-forth when submitting it. For a change of owner, the service-public.gouv.fr page lists the following documents:

- **The original registration certificate**, crossed out and signed by the seller with the words "Vendu le" (sold on) and the date.
- **The transfer certificate** (Cerfa 15776), filled in and signed by both parties.
- **A valid proof of identity**.
- **A proof of address** less than 6 months old.
- **Proof of vehicle inspection**, for a vehicle over 4 years old that is not exempt, less than 6 months old (2 months in case of a re-inspection).
- **The transfer code**, if it was provided by the seller.

The driving license and the proof of insurance are not attached: it is enough to certify on your honor that the owner holds them, and that the license matches the vehicle's category.

Scanning these documents now, in color and legible, makes it possible to start the application within minutes as soon as the website is back, or to send the file straight away to an authorized professional. To place the application in its broader context, our guide to [registering a vehicle online without going through a government office](/en/blog/online-vehicle-registration-without-government-office/) covers every step, and the [checklist for buying a used car](/en/blog/buying-used-car-checklist/) lists the documents to collect from the seller on the day of the sale.

A note for those moving house at the same time: the change of address follows its own timeline, covered in our article on the [legal deadline for a change of address](/en/blog/vehicle-registration-address-change-deadline/). It is done on a registration certificate already in the owner's name, so after the change of owner.

## Check that the outage really comes from the official website

Before switching approach, it is worth making sure the outage is real. A page that fails to load sometimes comes from the user's own device, not the service.

- **Switch browser or open a private window.** An extension, an ad blocker or an old cache can prevent some elements of the page from loading.
- **Switch connection.** Moving from Wi-Fi to mobile data helps distinguish a local network issue from an outage of the service.
- **Test at another time.** A page showing an error at peak time and working an hour later points to overload, not a lasting outage.

If the website remains unavailable on two different devices and two different connections, the outage really is on the service's side. This is the point where the choice comes up: wait if the deadline is far off, hand the application to an authorized professional if the vehicle needs to be back on the road quickly. cartegrise.com also publishes a guide on [handling your registration when the government website is not working](https://www.cartegrise.com/blog/2024/08/comment-faire-sa-demarche-de-carte-grise-quand-lants-ne-fonctionne-pas).

One last point: an authorized professional remains a private provider. cartegrise.com is authorized by the Ministry of the Interior and approved by the Treasury, but it is distinct from ANTS, whose official channel remains free aside from taxes.
