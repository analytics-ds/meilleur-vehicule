> **In brief:**
> 1. The coefficient decreases by 5% per year without an at-fault claim, down to a floor of 0.50.
> 2. Each at-fault claim increases the coefficient by 25%.
> 3. The no-claims bonus is transferable between insurers via the claims history statement.

## The principle of the bonus-malus coefficient

The no-claims bonus, officially the bonus-malus coefficient, adjusts the insurance premium based on the driver's at-fault claims history.

### The calculation mechanics

- -5% per full year without an at-fault claim
- +25% per at-fault claim
- +12.5% per partially at-fault claim

## Thresholds to know

| Situation | Effect on the coefficient |
|---|---|
| 13 years without an at-fault claim | Floor coefficient of 0.50 (maximum bonus) |
| 1 at-fault claim | Coefficient x 1.25 |
| Several claims in the same year | Cumulative increases |
| Maximum coefficient | Capped at 3.50 |

## How to lower your malus faster

1. Drive without an at-fault claim for at least 2 consecutive years
2. Compare offers, as the malus impact on pricing varies by insurer
3. Consider supervised driving or a driving awareness course, valued by some insurers

## A coefficient that follows the driver, not the vehicle

The bonus-malus coefficient stays attached to the insured person and is transferred to the new insurer via the claims history statement, including in case of [cancellation mid-year](/en/blog/switching-car-insurance-mid-year-cancellation/).

It takes on average 2 claim-free years to erase the effect of a single accident, and up to 13 consecutive years to reach the maximum bonus.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Is the no-claims bonus transferable between insurers?</summary>

Yes, the coefficient is attached to the driver: a claims history statement must be provided to the new insurer when switching companies.
