> **In brief:**
> 1. The new owner has a legal deadline of **1 month** after the purchase to register the vehicle in their name, against **15 days** for the seller to declare the sale.
> 2. Two legal routes exist: the **official agency website** (free aside from the tax, self-service process) or a **provider authorized** by the Ministry of the Interior (service fee, file checked before submission).
> 3. The regional tax amount is **identical** whichever route is chosen: only the service around the file changes, never the price set by the State and the region.
> 4. A flat-rate fine of **135 euros** sanctions a registration that has not been updated in case of a roadside check.

## What a vehicle registration transfer actually is

A **vehicle registration transfer**, also called a change of ownership, is the process that officially transfers a used vehicle to its new buyer within the French vehicle registration system (SIV). It follows every sale, whether between private individuals or through a dealer, and it is distinct from a simple **address update on the vehicle registration**, which only changes the owner's contact details without transferring ownership.

Until this process is finalized, the vehicle stays administratively linked to its previous owner, including for the delivery of traffic tickets for offenses committed by the new driver.

### Two separate obligations, two different deadlines

The process actually involves two separate declarations, carried out by two different people:

- The **seller** must declare the sale online within **15 days** of the transaction, which generates a transfer code to hand over to the buyer.
- The **buyer** must then have a new registration certificate issued in their name within **1 month** of the purchase date stated on the transfer certificate.

## The documents to gather before starting

Whether the process is done directly on the official agency website or through an authorized provider, the list of required documents is **identical**. An incomplete file is the leading cause of rejection and delay, regardless of the route chosen.

### List of required documents

| Document | Detail |
|---|---|
| Transfer certificate | Official form signed by both the seller and the buyer, handed over at the time of sale |
| Former registration certificate | Cancelled, with the handwritten note "sold on [date] at [time]" and the seller's signature |
| Proof of identity | Valid ID card, passport or residence permit |
| Proof of address | Less than 6 months old, in the new owner's name |
| Administrative status certificate | Less than 15 days old, confirms the vehicle is free of any lien or seizure |
| Roadworthiness test | Less than 6 months old if the vehicle is more than 4 years old, unless exempt |

## The legal deadline and the risks of being late

Respecting the 1 month deadline is not a simple administrative recommendation: it is an obligation set by the French **traffic code**, and failing to meet it is sanctioned.

### What the new owner risks

- A **flat-rate fine of 135 euros** during a check if the registration no longer matches the vehicle's actual driver
- A possible increase of the fine if it is not paid within the deadline stated on the notice
- Difficulty proving good faith if disputing a ticket received during the transition period, as long as the registration in the new owner's name is not finalized

According to Service-public.gouv.fr, the official French administrative information website, declaring a change of ownership is an obligation that applies "within one month of acquiring the vehicle" (Direction de l'information légale et administrative, 2024). This legal deadline is the basis for every timeframe mentioned in this article.

## Official agency or authorized provider: the two legal routes

This is the most misunderstood part of the process: **there is no single official route and an alternative one**, but two equally legal channels for the same formality, one handled directly by the State, the other by private providers holding an authorization from the Ministry of the Interior to process files on its behalf.

### Comparing the two options

| Criteria | Official agency website | Authorized provider |
|---|---|---|
| Service cost | Free, only the regional tax remains due | Variable service fee depending on the provider, in addition to the tax |
| Support | None, the owner fills in and scans the file alone | File checked before submission |
| Risk of rejection | Higher if a document is missing or poorly readable | Reduced thanks to the prior check |
| Regional tax amount | Identical | Identical |
| Provider status | State administration | Provider authorized by the Ministry of the Interior, distinct from the State |

An authorized provider, such as [Cartegrise.com](https://www.cartegrise.com/), is never the official agency itself: it is a professional authorized by the Ministry of the Interior to process registration files on its behalf, which makes it a distinct legal route, not an unofficial alternative. The difference between the two channels never concerns the tax amount, set by the State and the region, only the service provided around the file.

## The cost of a registration transfer

The amount to pay is made up of several components, only one of which really varies depending on the vehicle and the owner's region of residence.

### What makes up the bill

- The **regional tax**, calculated by multiplying the vehicle's fiscal horsepower by the fiscal horsepower rate set each year by the regional council of the owner's residence, a rate that varies noticeably from one region to another
- The **delivery fee** for the new document, around 2.76 euros, identical everywhere in France
- The **service fee** of the authorized provider, only if this route is chosen, variable depending on the provider

No ecological penalty applies to a simple ownership transfer on a used vehicle already registered in France: that mechanism concerns first registrations of new or imported vehicles, not resales between private individuals.

## The practical steps of the process

1. **Get the transfer certificate** signed by the seller at the time of purchase, along with the transfer code issued after their online declaration
2. **Gather the supporting documents** listed above, making sure they are readable if scanned
3. **Choose the processing route**: self-service filing on the official agency website, or submission to an authorized provider for a file check
4. **Pay the regional tax** corresponding to the vehicle's fiscal horsepower
5. **Receive the new registration certificate**, within a timeframe ranging from a few days to several weeks depending on demand and how complete the file is, with a temporary certificate allowing the vehicle to be driven in the meantime

For a vehicle sold between private individuals, the **[mandatory documents for selling a car privately](/en/blog/selling-car-private-mandatory-documents/)** must have been handed over in full by the seller before the buyer can even start this process: without a transfer certificate or a cancelled registration, neither the official agency nor an authorized provider can complete the file. For a first registration unrelated to buying a vehicle already on the road, the **[online vehicle registration](/en/blog/online-vehicle-registration-without-government-office/)** process follows a similar path but with different documents. And if the new owner moves shortly after the purchase, they will then need to follow the **[vehicle registration address change](/en/blog/vehicle-registration-address-change-deadline/)** procedure, which is separate from the one described here.
