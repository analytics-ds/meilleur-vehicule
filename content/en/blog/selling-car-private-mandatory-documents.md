> **In brief:**
> 1. 4 documents are mandatory: transfer certificate, crossed-out registration certificate, no-liens certificate, roadworthiness test.
> 2. The seller has 15 days to declare the transfer after the sale.
> 3. Without declaration, the seller remains liable for offences committed by the new buyer.

## Documents to hand over to the buyer

### The transfer certificate

The official transfer certificate is a mandatory document, filled in and signed in two copies: one for the seller, one for the buyer.

- Official transfer certificate, in 2 copies
- Crossed-out, dated and signed registration certificate
- Administrative status certificate less than 15 days old
- Roadworthiness test less than 6 months old (vehicle over 4 years)

## Declaring the transfer online

### A 15-day deadline to respect

| Step | Deadline | Who does it |
|---|---|---|
| Online transfer declaration | 15 days after the sale | The seller |
| Registration in their name | 1 month after the purchase | The buyer |
| Handing over the no-liens certificate | The day of the sale | The seller |

## Why this deadline matters

Without a transfer declaration, the seller remains legally liable for any use of the vehicle, including traffic offences committed by the new owner.

## Handling the paperwork without traveling

These procedures can be completed entirely online, without visiting a government office in person. Providers officially authorized by the State, such as [Cartegrise.com](https://www.cartegrise.com/), let you submit the transfer file and get an acknowledgment within minutes. ## Frequently asked questions

<details>
<summary>What documents are mandatory to sell your car privately?</summary>

You must provide the official transfer certificate, the crossed-out registration certificate, a no-liens certificate less than 15 days old, and the roadworthiness test if applicable.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Do you have to declare the sale of the vehicle to the registration agency?</summary>

Yes, the seller must declare the transfer within 15 days of the sale, to be released from liability in case of an offence committed by the new buyer.

</details>

<details>
<summary>What does a seller risk by not declaring the transfer of their vehicle?</summary>

They remain liable for fines and offences committed with the vehicle after the sale, until the new owner registers the car in their name.

</details>
